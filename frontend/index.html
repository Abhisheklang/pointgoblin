How a planning poker session runs

Five beats
  1. 01

    Open the room

    The host creates a room and adds the stories the team wants to size.

  2. 02

    Share one code

    Teammates join as guests with the six character room code. No account, no install.

  3. 03

    Vote in secret

    Everyone picks a card from the classic deck: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, a question mark, or a pass.

  4. 04

    Reveal together

    The host reveals. PointGoblin shows every vote with the average and the agreement level.

  5. 05

    Talk, then move on

    The team talks about the gaps, votes again if needed, and moves to the next story.

Why hidden votes make estimates better

Fairness

Fair by design

Anchoring is the quiet problem in sprint planning. One confident guess pulls every later guess toward it. Hidden cards remove that pull, so quiet engineers count as much as loud ones.

Consensus

Consensus you can see

The reveal shows more than an average. The agreement level tells the team whether the number is settled or whether the spread hides a misunderstanding worth a short conversation.

Speed

Fast enough for real calls

A room opens in seconds and every vote appears the moment it is cast. There is nothing to install and nothing to configure, which keeps the ceremony inside the planning call where it belongs.

Planning poker questions, answered

Good to know
What is planning poker?

Planning poker is an estimation technique from agile software development. The team discusses a story, then each person secretly picks a card with a story point value. All cards are revealed together and the team agrees on one estimate.

Do guests need an account to vote?

No. Only the host needs an account. Guests join with the room code and a display name, and they can vote right away.

Why does the deck jump from 8 to 13 to 21?

The deck follows a rounded growth sequence. Bigger work is harder to size precisely, so the gaps between cards grow with the numbers. It keeps the team honest about uncertainty.

What do average and agreement mean?

The average is the mean of the numeric votes, ignoring question marks and passes. Agreement is the share of numeric votes that match the most common value. High agreement means the team already sees the story the same way.
